Five things he will say in the first ten minutes.

His short version: AI is still underhyped, and overhyped. It depends which floor of the building you are on.

  1. AI is underhyped in the back office and overhyped in the boardroom.

    The boring workflows, invoices, reporting, operations, pay back first.

  2. Mid-sized companies have the AI advantage now.

    Fewer layers and faster decisions beat bigger budgets.

  3. Build versus buy has flipped.

    With AI, a custom internal tool often costs less than another fifty SaaS seats.

  4. The most expensive thing in tech isn't developers. It's slow decisions.

  5. Growth is an engineering problem now.

    The teams that win at go-to-market instrument everything and test weekly.

The short version of a long story.

Tengo is the Georgian short form of Tengiz. He was born and raised in Manchester to a Georgian family, and caught the business bug early: he sold sweets in the school playground to fund a World Challenge expedition to Argentina. He idolised Silicon Valley as a teenager.

Cambridge made him an engineer. Curiosity about how other industries worked took him through finance and into McKinsey. A bank's CEO took him aside after a final presentation and asked him to join, so he did. Then he went back to startups, and then he started his own. Along the way he founded Digital Institute, a tech school taught by MAANG lecturers whose students went on to Tesla, Google and other top companies. It has since closed.

Off the clock he plays bits of piano (Grade 8), still lifts, hikes when the map allows, plays padel, travels more than is sensible, and takes food seriously. In London he will send you to Brasserie Zédel, Dal Fiorentino, and a bar literally called "Bar with shapes as a name". Tbilisi has a whole document. Miami is too new to judge.

Curiosity, adventure, excellence. In that order on a good day.
